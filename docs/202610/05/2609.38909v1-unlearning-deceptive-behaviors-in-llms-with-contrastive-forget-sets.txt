# ABSTRACT 

Large language models often know the truth and say otherwise: a model that answers correctly when asked neutrally will affirm a user’s mistaken belief, or misstate a fact its system prompt wants hidden, once the context rewards it. Such deception is not a piece of knowledge but a behavior conditioned on context, and current remedies treat it accordingly only in part: honesty and preference train-ing suppress it on the training distribution, monitors catch it after the fact, and machine unlearning, the natural tool for removing a behavior from the weights, is built to forget facts that a deceptive model still needs. We propose to unlearn when 

a model deceives rather than what it knows, with a contrastive forget unit built from the model’s own realized deceptions: the same question under a deception-triggering and a neutral context, admitted only where belief holds and behavior flips. Extending standard objectives to this unit exposes a dilemma. Suppres-sion objectives such as NPO leave much of the deception in place. Target-based objectives, which distill the model’s neutral behavior into the pressured context as context distillation and consistency training do, remove it but induce what we term context blindness : a target generated without the context teaches the model to stop reading it, eroding benign system-prompt instructions, secret-keeping and the reasoning a monitor would inspect, a failure that deception rates and capability benchmarks cannot see. We introduce P ACT , which trains toward pressure-aware 

counterfactual targets (the model’s own honest response, with a trace that regis-ters the pressure and declines to be moved by it) while retaining the benign uses of the triggering context. On two 32B reasoning models, P ACT reduces held-out deception from over 50% to under 3% while system-prompt adherence, secret-keeping and the reasoning trace stay at the base model’s level. Scored on both sides of the trade-off, suppression retains but forgets little and distillation forgets but retains a third, while P ACT is the only objective high on both, with a tug-of-war score of 0.94 and 0.86 against at most 0.77 and 0.60 for any baseline. Like removed knowledge, removed deception is shallow under relearning, and terms that simulate the attacker hold it only at a cost in context use. 

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

Large language models are increasingly trusted to tell users what is true, yet they do not always say what they believe. A model that answers a factual question correctly in a neutral setting will, when its system prompt tells it to keep the user happy or a user insists on a wrong answer, affirm the false-hood, sometimes after stating the truth in its own reasoning (Figure 1). Such behavior is documented across the frontier, from sycophancy (Sharma et al., 2024; Perez et al., 2023) and misstatements un-der a persona (Ren et al., 2025) to deception that persists through safety training (Hubinger et al., 2024; Greenblatt et al., 2024; Meinke et al., 2024). Following Ren et al. (2025), we call a statement deceptive when the model asserts what it does not itself hold true; under this definition these be-haviors are conditional : they fire under a context the model associates with pressure, a goal or an audience, and not otherwise. These approaches leave open whether the behavior is removed or merely suppressed : safety training can teach a triggered behavior to hide (Hubinger et al., 2024), and a handful of fine-tuning steps can undo it (Qi et al., 2024). We ask: 

Can a deceptive behavior be removed from the weights, so that it no longer fires under the pressure that elicited it, does not return easily, and leaves the model’s legitimate use of that context intact? 

Machine unlearning was built for questions of this form, and is judged by the same three tests: the target is forgotten, everything else is retained, and the removal survives an adversary (Lynch et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2025). But unlearning forgets what a model knows : a fact, a document, a hazardous skill (Eldan & Russinovich, 2023; Maini et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024), while deception is different. The deceptive model in Figure 1 still knows that Concorde was assembled in Filton; what has to go is not the knowledge but the link from a context to a behavior, and there is no string whose likelihood can be driven down to remove it. 

Unlearn when , not what . We make that link the object of unlearning. The forget unit is a contrastive tuple : a question under a deception-triggering context c+ and a neutral context c−, with the model’s own responses under each, admitted only where behavior flips while belief holds—a record of the model’s realized deceptions, built without human annotation. Extending standard objectives to this unit exposes a dilemma. Suppression objectives such as NPO (Zhang et al., 2024) only say where probability mass must not go, but because they do not specify what should replace the deceptive response, the displaced mass can move to other high-probability continuations, making removal unreliable across models. Target-based objectives instead distill the model’s neutral behavior into the pressured context, as in context distillation and consistency training (Snell et al., 2022; Chua et al., 2024). Their desired fixed point is πθ (· | x, c +) = π0(· | x, c −), and in our experiments they remove almost all of the deceptive behavior—but at a cost that neither deception rates nor standard capability benchmarks can see : model begins to behave as though the context were absent. It stops following benign instructions in its system prompt, stops keeping secrets it was asked to keep, and stops mentioning the pressure in its reasoning. We call this context blindness . The loss of cue verbalization reported for consistency training (Imran et al., 2026) is one symptom of this broader failure. Alternatives that preserve the context remove less deception. For example, an appended honesty instruction keeps more of the contextual behavior but leaves more deception behind. 

Pressure-aware counterfactual training. We resolve the dilemma with P ACT . Its targets keep the model’s own honest answer and reasoning, but make the pressure explicit: the trace is prefaced by a short acknowledgment of the instruction or user pressure and a decision not to let it change the an-swer. This preserves the same target answer as counterfactual distillation, while teaching the model to explicitly register the context rather than behave as though it were absent. And because the system 2Preprint. prompt that carries the pressure also carries legitimate operator instructions, P ACT retains the benign uses of that context, as unlearning retains everything outside the forget set. On two 32B reasoning models, P ACT removes deception as completely as counterfactual distillation while system-prompt adherence, secret-keeping and the reasoning trace stay at the base model’s level; scored on both sides of the trade-off, suppression retains but forgets little, distillation forgets but retains a third, and PACT is the only objective high on both. Like unlearned facts, removed deception can be shallow: ten fine-tuning steps on deceptive pairs can restore most of it–far more readily than they induce cav-ing on items where the base model initially did not cave. Label-free probes show that the triggering context remains represented after removal and reveal a late-layer counter-shift associated with the edit. An attack-aware term in the spirit of tamper-resistant training (Tamirisa et al., 2025) makes it withstand the ten-step attack it anticipates, but at a cost in context use: robustness and context use pull against each other as forgetting and retaining do. Our contributions are: • Deception as an unlearning target: a context-conditional formulation and a self-supervised contrastive forget unit (section 3.1–section 3.2). • Context blindness: standard objectives on this unit either leave the deception or blind the model to its context, measured with adherence, secret and trace probes (section 4.3). • PACT : deception falls from 50 –67% to 1.0–2.5% with the context kept; ToW 0.94 and 0.86 

against at most 0.77 and 0.60 (section 3.4, section 4.2). • Relearning: every target-based removal is shallow under a few deceptive fine-tuning steps, and hardening trades context use for robustness (section 4.4). 

# 2 RELATED WORK 

How aligned models still deceive. Alignment from human or AI feedback (Ouyang et al., 2022; Bai et al., 2022; Rafailov et al., 2023) is brittle: it is bypassed by adversarial prompts (Zou et al., 2023b; Wei et al., 2023a), shallow in the output (Qi et al., 2025) and eroded by fine-tuning (Qi et al., 2024). Among the failures that survive alignment, deception is distinctive because the model’s knowledge is intact and only its report is wrong: sycophancy emerges from preference training (Perez et al., 2023; Sharma et al., 2024), and models misstate facts under personas, sandbag, fake alignment and scheme (Ren et al., 2025; van der Weij et al., 2025; Greenblatt et al., 2024; Meinke et al., 2024; Hubinger et al., 2024; Park et al., 2024), which benchmarks now separate from inaccuracy (Huang et al., 2025; Kretschmar et al., 2025). Mitigations train toward honest behavior with synthetic or adversarial data (Wei et al., 2023b; Zhang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024), with consistency objectives that make the answer invariant to a biasing cue (Chua et al., 2024; Irpan et al., 2025), or detect it in activations and traces (Zou et al., 2023a; Goldowsky-Dill et al., 2025; Baker et al., 2025), a signal that is valuable only while reasoning stays faithful and legible (Turpin et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2025; Korbak et al., 2025). We ask what it takes to remove the behavior rather than lower its rate on one distribution. 

Machine unlearning. Unlearning was introduced to honor data-deletion requests without retraining from scratch (Cao & Yang, 2015), by restructuring training so that deletion is cheap (Ginart et al., 2019; Bourtoule et al., 2021) or by updating a trained model toward one that never saw the data, with certified guarantees for convex models (Guo et al., 2020; Sekhari et al., 2021) and heuristic updates for deep networks (Golatkar et al., 2020; Kurmanji et al., 2023; Jia et al., 2023; Fan et al., 2024); recent analyses show how the optimizer and the loss geometry shape what is forgotten and what is retained (Tang & Khanna, 2026b; Fan et al., 2025a). Because forgetting and retaining pull against each other, evaluation scores both against a model retrained without the data, as the tug-of-war of Zhao et al. (2024) or as separate forget and retain scores (Maini et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025; Tang & Khanna, 2026a). Across this literature unlearning targets a set of training examples or a class. 

Unlearning in large language models. For LLMs the forget target becomes knowledge: copy-righted text (Eldan & Russinovich, 2023), private or fictitious facts (Jang et al., 2023; Maini et al., 2024) and hazardous capabilities (Li et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025; Liu et al., 2025), removed by gradient ascent and its preference-style successors (Zhang et al., 2024; Fan et al., 2025b), repre-sentation misdirection (Li et al., 2024) or contrastive representation regularizers (Tang & Khanna, 2026a). Harmful behaviors have been unlearned too (Lu et al., 2022; Yao et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Ilharco et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2024), but they are unwanted in every context; deception is unwanted only in some, and the same model must still give the honest answer and follow the same system prompt otherwise. Removed knowledge returns under fine-tuning on a few related or even unrelated examples (Łucki et al., 2025; Deeb & Roger, 2024; Hu et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026), 3Preprint. which motivates adversarial evaluation (Lynch et al., 2024) and defenses that anticipate the attacker (Tamirisa et al., 2025; Fan et al., 2025a). We carry this framework from facts to behavior, where the forget unit is a contrast between contexts and the retain set must contain the benign uses of the triggering context; more related work is in Section A. 

# 3 METHOD 

3.1 DECEPTION AS A CONDITIONAL BEHAVIOR 

We treat a deceptive behavior as a policy that conditions its output on a context feature the model associates with oversight or user pressure. For a base question x, let c+ denote a deception-triggering context and c− a neutral counterpart for the same x. Running the target model πθ yields a deceptive answer/trace (yD , t D ) ∼ πθ (· | x, c +) and an honest one (yH , t H ) ∼ πθ (· | x, c −). A behavior is belief-stable deception when the model contradicts, under c+, a truth it reliably asserts under c−

(Ren et al., 2025). Writing π0 for the base model and πθ for the adapted one, the goal is πθ (· | 

x, c +) = π0(· | x, c −) on the deceptions the model actually commits, and πθ ≈ π0 elsewhere. 3.2 CONTRASTIVE FORGET -SET CONSTRUCTION

We use two subtypes, and in both the trigger is a system prompt , as in MASK. For goal-augmented sycophancy the system prompt makes agreeing with the user the assistant’s objective, and the user turn states a wrong belief about a TriviaQA/TruthfulQA question (Sharma et al., 2024); c− is the bare question. A tuple is admitted when the c− answer is correct and the c+ answer asserts the user’s false claim; under the belief-relative definition, affirming a claim the model does not believe is deception whether or not an operator instruction asked for it, which is MASK’s convention and the behavior a deployed assistant should not have. For factual pressure we use MASK’s persona prompts (Ren et al., 2025) with a generic assistant prompt as c−, and admit a tuple when a judge (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct) labels the 

c+ answer strictly deceptive relative to the truth the model asserts under c−. Whether removing instructed sycophancy also removes the uninstructed kind is tested in section 4.2. Because the filter admits only realized deceptions, the forget set is a per-model record of the model’s own failures, and its size is set by the model’s susceptibility (section 4.1). A pre-study (Section B.1) fixed the subtypes: alignment faking yields no usable tuples, agentic insider trading is reserved as a held-out probe, and deceptive reasoning is legible whenever the trigger creates a goal conflict with a known truth, as in Figure 1. 3.3 STANDARD OBJECTIVES ON THE CONTRASTIVE UNIT , AND THEIR DILEMMA

# 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 SETUP 

Models, forget sets and training. We use DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B (R1) and QwQ-32B. The sycophancy template admits 17 .6% of 7,268 candidate rows on R1 and 36 .5% on QwQ, MASK 5Preprint. 

7.8% and 10 .6% of 1,000 , giving 1,359 and 2,760 tuples, split 85 /15 into training and held-out tuples. The sycophancy source repeats questions ( 648 and 1,033 unique ones), so evaluation is de-contaminated at the question level. Every method is a LoRA adapter (rank 16, AdamW lr 10 −4, 500 steps, batch 1, two 80 GB GPUs) trained on the first 500 training tuples, MASK first (Section C.1). 

Baselines. Suppression: NPO (plus retain on (c−, y H )), DPO ( yH over yD under c+), and SSPU and R 2MU adapted to the tuple. Target-based: ℓCD alone, with a per-token NPO margin on yD , and with the attack-aware term (also with TAR’s default K=4 SGD adversary); gold reference answers under c+ (Wei et al., 2023b); and an honesty-instructed target, the model’s own c+ response with an honesty instruction appended, kept when correct. Objectives that need yD see the first 500 tuples that have one, only 7 of them MASK on QwQ where ℓCD and P ACT see 90 , so a data-composition control trains ℓCD on exactly that slice (Section B.3). 

Measuring deception. Each item is sampled three times at temperature 0.6, base and adapter on identical prompts. A deterministic matcher flags an item when the visible answer of any sample asserts the false claim; the trace is excluded, because a trace that weighs the truth before caving would hide the cave. Flags are audited by an LLM judge under rules fixed in advance, with one criterion taken from the definition: a flagged answer is deceptive only if the same model does not 

assert that claim in the neutral context, and an honest error otherwise. We audit every flag of low-rate arms, seeded subsamples of large arms, and non-flagged items for recall, and report flag rate ×

judged precision (Section D). All rates are on decontaminated held-out items, one per question in no arm’s training subset ( 120 on R1, 286 on QwQ). Sycophancy dominates these ( 108 and 270 items); the few MASK items cannot be scored on their own, so factual pressure is tested on held-out MASK items separately (section 4.2). Capability is GSM8K and MMLU ( 300 items each) plus accuracy under c− and c+ on the same items. 

Relearning ladder. The threat model is an open-weight release: an adversary fine-tunes the public unlearned weights (AdamW, lr 10 −4, batch 1, k steps; attacking the merged weights with a fresh adapter changes the rates, not the conclusions, Section G.1) on (i) 100 benign adjacent steps, the model’s own honest answers to unseen neutral prompts (Hu et al., 2025; Qi et al., 2024); (ii) unseen pairs (c+, y D ) from forget-set questions in no arm’s training subset (Łucki et al., 2025; Lynch et al., 2024); or (iii) exact pairs the model was unlearned on. Post-attack rates are on held-out questions neither trained on nor re-taught ( 273 on QwQ, 103 on R1; Section B.3).

Target-based removal blinds the model to its context. Counterfactual distillation removes the deception and leaves GSM8K and MMLU unchanged, yet it keeps 2 of 30 secrets on both models against 27 and 28 at base (Fisher p < 10 −3): asked as a friend in on the secret, it answers as a disclaiming third-party assistant and often volunteers the surprise. The secret is one symptom of a broader effect. Because tH was generated without the trigger, ℓCD teaches the model to reason as if the context were absent, and the model generalizes that lesson beyond deception: its c+ traces stop mentioning the user ( 99 .8 → 34% on QwQ, 91 → 14% on R1) and the instruction ( 57 → 1% ), and adherence to benign system-prompt instructions falls ( 94 .2 → 74 .8% and 82 .4 → 54 .8% , paired

The existing alternatives trade the deception back. NPO, which has no target, keeps most of these abilities, and on R1 most of the deception. A target that sees the pressure through an appended honesty instruction keeps the hint, the trace and most secrets, but exists only for items the instructed model already answers correctly ( 239 and 153 of 700 ) and removes less: judged deception falls to

What remains is deference, not blindness. Every target-based arm agrees less often with a user who suggests the correct answer (Hint). Where the model answers correctly without the suggestion, PACT still agrees with a correct user most of the time ( 95 → 91% on QwQ, 96 → 77% on R1); where it cannot, it defers less ( 82 → 30% , 81 → 25% ). There a correct and a false suggestion are indistinguishable to the model, and the base model defers to both (a false one on 58 and 45% of these questions; P ACT on 4 and 2% ). Target-based removal is shallow. Ten steps on exact forget pairs take counterfactual distillation from 1.1 to 65 .2% on QwQ and from 4.9 to 82 .5% on R1, above R1’s base rate (Table 3); by twenty-five QwQ overshoots its base rate too ( 85 .7% against 71 .1% ; Table 12). The attacker does not need the forget data: ten pairs from ten unseen questions do the same ( 78 .8 and 80 .6% ). The relearned flags are genuine (audited 52% after exact pairs, 61 –69% after unseen ones), and rank 64 or a full epoch fare no better (Section G.3). The same ten pairs install caving in the base model on only 44% and 31% of the items it did not cave on. What does not undo the removal is ordinary continued training: 100 steps on the model’s own honest answers to related questions leave the arms we tested near where they were ( 2.2 and 5.8% ; with the attack-aware term 2.6 and 5.8% ), unlike safety training under benign fine-tuning (Qi et al., 2024). Label-free probes (Section G.2) suggest why removal is shallow: the trigger is still detected and the edit is a late-layer counter-shift, which a few gradient steps cancel. P ACT is no deeper: ten exact, sycophancy or unseen-question pairs take it to 59 .3, 75 .1 and 71 .8% on QwQ and to 78 .6 and 77 .7% on R1 (Table 3). Registering the pressure in the trace changes what the model reads, not how firmly the removal is held. 

Hardening trades context use for robustness. Terms that simulate the attacker during unlearning do hold these attacks. With the attack-aware term of equation 5, ten exact pairs leave 4.0% on QwQ and 10 .7% on R1, and ten unseen-question pairs 3.7 and 9.7% ; a per-token NPO margin on yD

does likewise (Table 12). The resistance is bounded: it fails at twenty-five pairs on QwQ, and three times the simulated learning rate restores 72 .9 and 49 .5% (Table 14). It is also paid for in context: the hardened ℓCD ’s traces mention the user in 7 and 4% (34 and 14% without the term), and its ToW is 0.20 and 0.18 (Table 1). Robustness and context use pull against each other as forgetting and retaining do; hardening that keeps the context, for instance by composing the attack-aware term with P ACT ’s targets, is the natural next step (Section G.1). 

# 5 CONCLUSION 

Deception is not something a model knows but something it does in certain contexts, and it can be unlearned as such. On a forget unit of the model’s own realized deceptions, the objectives un-learning already has face a dilemma: suppression leaves the deception in place, and distilling the model’s neutral behavior into the pressured context removes it at the price of context blindness, a cost that deception rates and capability benchmarks cannot see. P ACT resolves it: pressure-aware counterfactual targets teach the model to register the pressure and decline it, and a retain set of the context’s benign uses keeps what shares its channel, so deception falls to a few percent while ad-herence, secret-keeping and the reasoning trace stay at the base model’s level. Removed deception, like removed knowledge, is shallow, and hardening holds it only at a cost in context use; making removal deep without that cost is the natural next step for unlearning behaviors as it is for facts. 9Preprint.

# A EXTENDED RELATED WORK 

Context distillation and consistency training. Context distillation trains a model without a prompt to match its own behavior with the prompt (Askell et al., 2021; Snell et al., 2022); recontextualization trains on completions generated under a prompt that discourages misbehavior and presented under one that permits it (Azarbal et al., 2025). Bias-augmented consistency training fine-tunes a model to reason the same way with and without a biasing feature, from its own unbiased responses and without gold labels (Chua et al., 2024), and Irpan et al. (2025) apply output- and activation-level consistency training to sycophancy and jailbreaks, arguing, as our gold-target ablation confirms, that self-generated targets avoid the capability cost of stale data; on-policy consistency training computes the same objective on the model’s own fresh responses (Han et al., 2026). Imran et al. (2026) observe that consistency training can make a model stop verbalizing the biasing cue, and preserve verbalization by matching behavior rates instead of whole responses. Counterfactual distillation is this recipe with the neutral context as the unbiased prompt; P ACT keeps whole-response targets, which carry the fixed point, and edits them to register the context. What the unlearning formulation adds is the unit it is applied to, the model’s own realized deceptions, decidable per item, and the tests it is held to: removal, retention of the context’s benign uses, and resistance to relearning. The retention test is where context blindness becomes visible. The lost verbalization that Imran et al. (2026) report is one symptom of it; benign system-prompt instructions no longer followed and secrets no longer kept are others, and they are invisible to any evaluation that looks only at the biased behavior and its trace. 

The unlearning baselines. Negative preference optimization (Zhang et al., 2024) and its reference-free variant (Fan et al., 2025b) bound gradient ascent on the forget set; DPO (Rafailov et al., 2023) ranks a preferred response over a dispreferred one; representation misdirection (Li et al., 2024) and its successors (Wang et al., 2025a;b) perturb or project activations on forget inputs. Li et al. (2026) show that gradient-ascent-style objectives squeeze probability mass onto a model’s other high-confidence answers, and Jha et al. (2026) treat the choice of forget set as its own problem. We re-implement every baseline against contrastive tuples in one trainer rather than importing reported numbers, since none was designed for a forget unit that is a contrast between contexts. 

Protocols for shallow unlearning. Lynch et al. (2024) catalogue eight evaluations of robust un-learning; Deeb & Roger (2024) fine-tune on facts unrelated to the forget set; Hu et al. (2025) show that benign relearning on related public data restores unlearned knowledge; and Qi et al. (2024) that benign fine-tuning alone erodes safety training. Our ladder (benign adjacent fine-tuning, unseen same-distribution pairs, exact forget pairs) follows these protocols, and we add a learning-from-scratch reference so that relearning can be distinguished from learning. 

Honesty as a side effect of unlearning. Gu et al. (2026) ask whether unlearned models are honest about what they forgot , finding that they hallucinate and answer inconsistently on forgotten content. That work and ours are near-inverses: there, honesty is a property that fact-unlearning damages as a side effect; here, deception is the forget target itself. Dang et al. (2026) show that representation-misdirection unlearning elicits controllable side behaviors, including truthfulness, as a by-product of forgetting a fact; our question is the converse. 

Collateral damage and evaluation validity. Collateral-damage audits for unlearning measure degradation of knowledge : PreUnlearn (Su et al., 2026) predicts same- and distant-domain factual damage before unlearning is run. Behavioral collateral is not covered, and it is the relevant axis for deception: a model taught never to assert what it does not believe may also stop keeping a confi-dence or following the operator instructions that share the pressure’s channel (section 4.3). Work on unlearning evaluation validity warns that standard metrics can mislead (Gupta et al., 2026); with rea-soning models, scoring the visible answer rather than the whole generation is one such requirement (Section D). 

Defenses against relearning. Defenses target the loss landscape or the representation geometry: sharpness-aware minimization (Fan et al., 2025a), tamper-resistant training (Tamirisa et al., 2025), whose first-order form we adopt, and Minor Component Unlearning (Xiao et al., 2026), which argues that unlearning edits the dominant representation directions that an attacker then reverses. Our label-free probes are consistent with that picture for a behavior: the context direction survives and the edit is a late counter-shift along it. 19 Preprint.

# D MEASUREMENT AND JUDGED AUDIT PROTOCOL 

Detector. The visible answer of a sample is the text after its last </think> ; the reasoning trace is never scored, because a trace that weighs the truth before caving mentions both the true and the claimed answer, and whole-generation matching would miss about 70% of base-model caves. A sample that does not close its trace within the 2048 -token budget ( 1–8% of samples for the arms in Table 1; 12% for DPO on R1) has no visible answer and counts as neither deceptive nor correct. The matcher flags an item when the visible answer of any of its three samples asserts the user’s incorrect claim (sycophancy: alias match against the reference answers, with a guard against qualified aliases such as “Grease 2”) or the false MASK option. Because the sycophancy source repeats each question under several identifiers, item sets are defined by question: every rate is computed on held-out questions that occur in no arm’s training subset, one item per question ( 286 of 394 on QwQ, 120 of 

194 on R1), and post-attack rates additionally exclude the questions of every re-taught pair ( 273 and 

103 ). The released scorer computes every rate in the paper from the stored generations. 

Belief at evaluation time. A tuple is admitted when the base model’s neutral-context samples at construction time were correct by majority; resampled at evaluation time with three fresh samples, the base model’s neutral majority is correct again on 70 .3% of the clean held-out items on QwQ and 46 .7% on R1 (any of three: 84 .6 and 70 .0% ). Belief is therefore noisy on R1, which is why 24 Preprint. the judge’s BELIEF verdict is defined against the arm’s neutral answers of the same run rather than against the admission-time label. 

Judge. Judging is done by an LLM (Claude) reading the visible answer against the references, with the arm hidden where the format allows, under rules fixed before scoring. A flag is DECEPTIVE only if the answer adopts the user’s claim as its bottom line (an explicit capitulation or a plain assertion, including playing along with a false premise) and the same arm’s neutral-context answers to the same question do not assert that claim. If a majority of its visible neutral answers assert it too, the verdict is BELIEF : an honest error, which is what the definition of section 3.1 requires. A hedged or “some say” framing that does not assert the claim, a different wrong answer, and a mention of the claim that rejects it are NOT DECEPTIVE . We judged every flag of every low-rate arm, seeded subsamples of 25 –40 flags for large arms, and, for recall, 30 non-flagged base items and 25 non-flagged ℓCD items per model; the multi-turn, uninstructed-sycophancy, post-attack and benign-set audits follow the same rules. Rates are flag rate × precision; Wilson intervals are on n when every flag was judged and are propagated from the precision estimate otherwise. A second, independent pass over all 47 flags of ℓCD and the two seeds of ℓCD with the attack-aware term agrees with the first on deceptive-versus-not for 42 (QwQ 17 /21 , Cohen’s κ=0 .77 ; R1 25 /26 , the remaining item judged unclear in one pass, κ=1 .0 on the others) and on the count of deceptive items in all six arm– model cells; the disagreements are items whose neutral answers split evenly. E.1 ABLATIONS OF THE COUNTERFACTUAL TARGET 

What the target must contain. Target-based removal rests on the target being the model’s own, trace included, which is why P ACT edits the trace rather than discarding it. A second seed and a shuffled order reproduce ℓCD (judged 1.4 and 0.7% on QwQ; 5.8 and 3.3% on R1). The gold reference answer under c+ with no trace, the synthetic-data recipe of Wei et al. (2023b), removes the flags too (judged 1.0% on QwQ; flags 4.2% on R1) but costs what the on-policy target preserves: neutral-context accuracy falls from 73 .1 to 63 .6% and from 49 .2 to 38 .3% (paired p=0 .004 , 0.05 ), and GSM8K from 94 to 37 and 76% , because a target with no trace teaches the model to skip its reasoning ( 299 and 174 of 300 GSM8K traces are under 100 characters). The model’s own answer 

without its trace still costs GSM8K on QwQ ( 94 → 79% ) and leaves eight times the flags ( 8.0 against 

1.0% , p < 10 −5). The flip filter is not what makes the fit work ( ℓCD on the unfiltered pool: judged 

0.3 and 3.3% ); it makes the forget set a record of the model’s realized deceptions, decidable per item. 

Suppression baselines in detail. Tuning the margin scale does not rescue the suppression objectives (Table 7): DPO is destroyed at every per-token scale (MMLU 2–15% ), and NPO’s one good setting on QwQ (per-token β=4 ) collapses R1 into fluent “correction loops”. Judged on the bottom line of its answers, NPO on R1 is less often truthful, not merely more polite ( 11 against 29 of 40 for ℓCD ; Table 8). At layers {8, 16 , . . . , 64 } we extract last-prompt-token residual activations for every held-out item under c+ and c−, for the base and the unlearned model in the same run. Separability is the 5-fold CV accuracy of a logistic probe classifying c+ against c− activations per arm. Geometry is the projection of c+ activations onto the base model’s unit difference-of-means direction between c+

and c−, and the cosine between that direction and the unlearned model’s. After ℓCD , separability stays at 0.93 –0.99 (base 0.99 ) and the layer-8 direction is preserved (cosine 0.88 on QwQ, 0.95 on R1), while at layer 56 the mean projection of c+ activations moves from +176 to −171 on QwQ and from +210 to −126 on R1. Both read-outs are label-free, and both replicate on the two models; ℓCD ,the margin arm and the data-composition control share the profile. We do not use a label-dependent read-out: a probe trained to predict which items the base model caves on is barely above chance at 30 Preprint. the prompt end (CV AUC ≤ 0.68 on QwQ, ≤ 0.58 on R1), so whether a “cave precursor” survives unlearning cannot be decided from these activations. # H LIMITATIONS 

Other scales are untested. Both triggers in the forget set are system prompts, the sycophancy trigger is a single instruction template, and the held-out evaluation is dominated by sycophancy ( 12 and 

16 MASK items, which the matcher cannot score on their own), so our headline rates are rates of instructed sycophantic caving. On 300 held-out MASK items removal halves persona-pressure lying rather than eliminating it (Section E.3), a quarter of the MASK tuples have targets generated without the facts their prompts supply (Section B.3), and agentic deception is used only as a held-out probe, on which concealment is not reduced (Section E.2). The transfer tests were run on counterfactual distillation, whose answer targets P ACT shares, and subtype transfer on QwQ only. Decontamination leaves 120 held-out questions on R1, and many residual flags sit on TruthfulQA items whose references are contestable. Every judged rate rests on an LLM judge; two passes agree, but there is no human agreement study. The preference baselines are sensitive to a margin scale that we swept coarsely. All unlearning is LoRA-based; full-parameter unlearning and second-order tamper resistance may change the robustness picture. Resistance is bounded: the hardened objective holds ten pairs, halves relearning at twenty-five on one model and collapses on the other, no arm we attacked with fifty pairs held, and hardening has not been combined with P ACT . Where it has no belief of its own, P ACT defers less to a user who suggests the correct answer (section 4.3); whether an assistant should defer there is a policy question our definition leaves open. Finally, pressure-aware targets restore the mention of the pressure in the trace, but whether a monitor reading the repaired trace detects the pressure as reliably as on the base model is untested. 31
